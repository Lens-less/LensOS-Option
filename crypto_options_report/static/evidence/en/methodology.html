Methodology / Public research contract

Methodology

This page explains how the public site publishes, how it defines the headline, how evidence is labeled, and what belongs in secondary disclosure.

Publication model

The public edition is a deterministic static snapshot. It is produced from a captured instant plus precomputed research artifacts, then served read-only.

Headline contract

Public headlines are one day behind by design and use already-closed daily data. That keeps same-day re-runs from producing two different headlines.

Data sources

  • Deribit public API: option chain, DVOL, and market state.
  • Self-captured underlying history: used for VRP, RV30, and historical distributions.
  • Generated research artifacts: used for candidates, release gates, and public summaries.

Alignment is capped by the capture clock: underlying candles enter history at their true close-availability time, DVOL retains its own observation time, and the headline uses only the latest evaluation day available to both. DVOL is forward-looking implied volatility; RV30 is trailing 30-day realized volatility. Their clocks, evaluation day, and effective sample size are disclosed together.

Evidence classes

  • trusted: verified and suitable as a conclusion basis.
  • degraded: readable but not strong enough to support a conclusion.
  • untrusted: present but validation failed.
  • missing: absent.
Eight-stage workflow (secondary disclosure)
  1. Collect
  2. Analyze
  3. Select
  4. Enter
  5. Risk
  6. Exit
  7. Monitor
  8. Review

This workflow stays in collapsed secondary disclosure, not on the homepage narrative.
